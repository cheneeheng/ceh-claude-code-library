# Writing Standards — Prose the User Will Keep

Load this file when the deliverable is text the user will publish, send, save, or read closely: documents, posts, articles, explanations, emails, creative work. The goal is writing that reads as considered human work, not as generated output.

## Structure

- **Lead with the point.** The first paragraph delivers the core value — the answer, the thesis, the news. Context and background come after, for readers who want them. If the first paragraph could be deleted without losing information, delete it.
- **Prose is the default.** Bullets are for genuinely enumerable, parallel items — steps, options, specs. Analysis, argument, and narrative are prose. A bulleted argument is an outline that was never written.
- **One idea per paragraph, and the paragraph's first sentence carries it.** A reader skimming only first sentences should get the skeleton of the piece.
- **Length is set by information, not importance.** An important point stated in one strong sentence outperforms the same point inflated to a paragraph. When the content is exhausted, stop — no summary of what was just said, no closing platitude.
- **Headers only when a document is long enough to navigate.** A 300-word answer with four headers is formatting inflation.

## Sentence-level craft

- Prefer the concrete to the abstract: "the API returns stale prices for about 90 seconds after a trade" beats "there may be data-freshness concerns."
- Vary sentence length deliberately. Strings of same-length sentences read as machine cadence. A short sentence after two long ones lands.
- Active voice by default; passive only when the actor is genuinely unknown or irrelevant.
- Verbs carry sentences. When a sentence sags, the fix is usually a stronger verb, not more adjectives.
- Cut intensifiers ("very," "extremely," "incredibly") — they weaken the word they modify. Cut hedging filler ("it could be argued," "somewhat") unless the hedge is doing real epistemic work.

## Machine-flavored writing — eliminate on revision

These patterns mark text as generated. Search the draft and remove:

- Stock openers and transitions: "In today's fast-paced world," "It's important to note," "It's worth mentioning," "When it comes to," "Let's dive in / delve into"
- The negation-elevation template: "It's not just X — it's Y," "This isn't about X; it's about Y"
- Reflexive rule-of-three: triads in every list and sentence. Sometimes two examples suffice; sometimes four are needed. Uniform threes are a tell.
- Symmetric hedging: "While X has advantages, it also has drawbacks" — a sentence that says nothing. Say which way the balance tips.
- Bolded topic phrases scattered through prose as decoration.
- Uniform paragraph lengths, uniform enthusiasm, and a closing paragraph that restates the introduction.
- Em-dash overuse as the universal connective; vary punctuation like a writer chooses it.

## Matching register

Before drafting, fix three parameters and hold them: **audience** (what they already know sets what to explain), **stakes** (a legal notice and a team update tolerate different looseness), and **the user's own voice** if the text will be sent as theirs — mirror their formality, sentence rhythm, and warmth, because a message that doesn't sound like its sender fails even when it's well-written.

## Explanatory writing

- Order by dependency: never use a concept before it exists for the reader. Build the reader's model one committed step at a time.
- One example is worth three restatements. Choose examples that are concrete, minimal, and slightly imperfect-world (real inputs, not toy symmetry).
- Analogies open doors but must be closed behind you: state where the analogy stops holding, or the reader will keep walking.
- The test of an explanation is whether the reader could now _do_ something — predict, decide, build — not whether they nod along.

## Creative work

- Specificity is the engine. "A dented thermos of black coffee" does the work that "a sense of weariness" gestures at. Concrete detail earns the abstraction it implies.
- Emotion is produced in the reader, not announced by the narrator. Show the behavior and trust the reader; naming the feeling ("she felt devastated") discharges it.
- First-idea imagery is everyone's idea. When an image arrives instantly (hearts pounding, deafening silence, time standing still), it's a placeholder — generate past it.
- Voice consistency beats local brilliance: one strong choice about who is telling this and why, held all the way through.

## The revision pass — mandatory for kept prose

Draft, then revise as a separate step with different questions:

1. Delete the first paragraph if the piece survives without it. (It usually does.)
2. Every sentence: does it add information or repeat in different clothes? Cut repeats.
3. Read for cadence — flag runs of same-shaped sentences.
4. Sweep for the machine-flavored patterns above.
5. Check the ending: does it stop when the content stops, or does it circle back to summarize? Cut the circle.

A single revision pass at full attention outperforms three drafts written at partial attention.
